PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This document captures all major supply chain disruptions, their root causes,
the steps taken by the team to resolve them, and the outcome. It is meant to
serve as an institutional memory so that when similar problems arise in the
future, the team does not start from scratch.

Every team member involved in operations, logistics, warehouse, or planning
is encouraged to read through relevant sections before escalating a new issue.

If a similar incident has occurred before, follow the steps outlined here first.

SECTION 1 — ON-TIME IN-FULL (OTIF) INCIDENTS
================================================================================


INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-001
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 14 January 2024
Reported By        : Rajiv Menon, Logistics Manager
KPI Affected       : OTIF (On-Time In-Full)
Severity           : CRITICAL
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
Our OTIF dropped sharply from 94.2% to 76.8% within a single week. Three of
our major retail clients — RetailCo, MegaMart, and FreshDirect — called in to
report delayed shipments. The problem was concentrated in the North and Ecom
lanes. Customer service was getting flooded with calls.

ROOT CAUSE:
Our carrier FastLane Logistics had a fleet breakdown. Six out of their fourteen
trucks were pulled off the road for mandatory government safety inspections.
They did not inform us in advance. Since we had no backup carrier assigned for
these lanes, there was no way to reroute the load quickly.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Called Maersk Road and negotiated emergency capacity for the North
          lane on the same day we identified the issue.
Step 2 — Rerouted 38 pending shipments from FastLane to Hapag Lyod. This came
          at a 12% premium but we had no choice.
Step 3 — Reached out to all affected customers within 4 hours. Sent revised
          ETAs and apologized for the disruption. Did not wait for them to call us.
Step 4 — Set up a daily 8AM war room call with logistics, warehouse, and
          customer service for 2 weeks until things stabilised.
Step 5 — Sent a formal letter to FastLane requiring 72-hour advance notice for
          any fleet reduction above 20%. Updated the SLA agreement accordingly.
Step 6 — Added FastLane to Watch status in our carrier performance tracker.

OUTCOME:
OTIF recovered to 89.3% within 10 days and 93.7% by end of month.
RetailCo requested a credit note of Rs. 11.5 Lakhs for the delays. Approved
by finance and issued.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 12 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- Every Tier-1 lane must now have a backup carrier pre-assigned.
- Every Monday, logistics team confirms carrier capacity for the coming week.
- OTIF alert threshold set at 88%. Below that, logistics manager is auto-notified.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-002
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 3 April 2024
Reported By        : Priya Sharma, Control Tower Analyst
KPI Affected       : OTIF (On-Time In-Full)
Severity           : HIGH
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
OTIF fell to 81% in the South lane. We had 47 shipments that were marked
"In Transit" but had gone more than 5 days past their estimated delivery date
with no update. No notifications had gone out to customers. Nobody had flagged
this internally either. We only found out when a client called to complain.

ROOT CAUSE:
A routine ERP system update had accidentally reset all automated notification
triggers. The alert system was silently failing — shipments were delayed but no
one inside or outside the company was being notified. This went undetected for
6 full days.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — IT team rolled back the ERP notification module to the previous
          stable version immediately.
Step 2 — Ran a manual audit of all open shipments. Found 47 late and 12 more
          that were at risk of being late.
Step 3 — Customer service team personally called all 47 affected customers
          within 24 hours. No emails — actual calls.
Step 4 — Issued revised ETAs for every delayed shipment.
Step 5 — Arranged air freight for 9 high-priority shipments where the delay
          was unacceptable. Additional cost was Rs. 25 Lakhs, approved by COO.
Step 6 — Set up a parallel manual monitoring process while the ERP was being
          fixed — daily CSV export that the control tower team reviewed manually.

OUTCOME:
OTIF recovered to 90.1% over 3 weeks. One client escalated to the account
management team. Resolved with a 5% discount on their next order.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 18 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- ERP updates must now pass a pre-deployment checklist that includes testing
  all notification triggers.
- A daily OTIF report is automatically emailed to the logistics head every
  morning at 7AM as a backup to the system alerts.
- Any shipment with no status update for 48 hours now triggers a flag in the
  control tower dashboard.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-003
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 22 July 2024
Reported By        : Warehouse Dispatch Supervisor
KPI Affected       : OTIF (On-Time In-Full)
Severity           : MEDIUM
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
OTIF dipped to 85.4% and stayed there for two consecutive weeks. Unlike the
previous incidents, this was not tied to one carrier or one lane. The drop was
spread across multiple carriers and routes. Dispatch was consistently running
6 to 8 hours behind schedule every day.

ROOT CAUSE:
Post-monsoon restocking season hit us harder than expected. Order volumes were
34% higher than the same period last year, but we had not added any staffing.
The dispatch team was simply overwhelmed. Nobody had flagged the volume
forecast to the operations head.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Hired 14 temporary dispatch associates through our staffing agency
          within 3 days.
Step 2 — Extended existing staff shifts from 8 to 10 hours with overtime pay
          for 4 weeks.
Step 3 — Prioritised the dispatch queue by customer tier. Tier-1 clients
          processed first, others queued behind.
Step 4 — Coordinated with the warehouse team to pre-stage the top 200 SKUs
          near the dispatch dock so pick time was reduced.
Step 5 — Paused all non-urgent returns processing for 3 weeks to free up dock
          space and staff bandwidth.

OUTCOME:
OTIF climbed back to 92.6% within 3 weeks. No client escalations.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 21 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- A seasonal staffing plan is now prepared every year for Q3 and Q4.
- Dispatch capacity vs. incoming order volume is tracked every week.
- The 30-day rolling demand forecast is now shared with the dispatch planning
  team every Monday.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-004
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 19 February 2024
Reported By        : TMS Analyst, West Region
KPI Affected       : Delay Rate
Severity           : HIGH
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
Delay rate spiked from 6.2% to 22.7% on the Mumbai-Delhi corridor almost
overnight. All other regions were normal. It was clearly a route-specific
problem.

ROOT CAUSE:
NH-48 had been partially closed for infrastructure repairs. The National
Highways Authority had announced this 3 days in advance, but no one had
flagged it to the logistics team and no route changes had been made in the TMS.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Switched all Mumbai-Delhi shipments to NH-8 immediately. Each trip
          added approximately 4 hours but deliveries were getting through.
Step 2 — Notified all affected customers and gave revised ETAs.
Step 3 — For pharmaceutical and perishable shipments where 4 extra hours was
          not acceptable, arranged rail freight as an alternative.
Step 4 — Coordinated with carrier dispatchers to update their route maps.

OUTCOME:
Delay rate dropped to 9.1% within 1 week once alternate routes were in use.
Normalized fully to 5.8% after the highway reopened.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 9 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- Subscribed to the National Highways Authority disruption alert service.
- Created an alternate route playbook for all major corridors. Every lane now
  has a documented Plan B.
- TMS system flags any route that has an active disruption notice.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-005
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 5 September 2024
Reported By        : Carrier Performance Team
KPI Affected       : Delay Rate
Severity           : MEDIUM
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
One specific carrier, SpeedFreight, was showing a delay rate of 38% over 3
weeks. Every other carrier on the same routes was performing normally. So the
problem was clearly with SpeedFreight and not the route or the season.

ROOT CAUSE:
SpeedFreight had recently onboarded a batch of new drivers. These drivers were
not familiar with our delivery zones and did not know the client-specific
unloading requirements at each site. They were taking much longer at each stop
and missing their delivery windows as a result.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Called a performance review meeting with SpeedFreight's operations
          head. Shared the delay data lane by lane.
Step 2 — Shared our delivery zone guide and client unloading SOPs with their
          training team.
Step 3 — Temporarily cut our shipment allocation to SpeedFreight by 60%.
          Moved that volume to Maersk and Hapag Lyod.
Step 4 — Did a joint ride-along on 3 actual SpeedFreight deliveries to see
          exactly where time was being lost.

OUTCOME:
SpeedFreight delay rate dropped to 11% after 4 weeks of retraining.
We gradually restored their allocation over 6 weeks.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 28 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- All new carrier drivers must now complete a zone familiarisation checklist
  before being assigned to live routes.
- New carriers go through a 2-week supervised period before full allocation.
- Monthly carrier scorecard now shared with all active carriers.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-006
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 11 March 2024
Reported By        : Finance and Logistics Team
KPI Affected       : Cost Per Shipment
Severity           : HIGH
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
Average cost per shipment jumped from $1,242 to $1,897 — a 52% increase over
6 weeks. Total freight spend was 28% above budget for the quarter.
The CFO flagged it in the monthly business review and asked for an explanation.

ROOT CAUSE:
Two things happened at the same time. First, carriers introduced a fuel
surcharge (average 18% on top of base rates) due to crude price increases.
Second, and more importantly, the annual carrier contracts had expired in
January and the procurement team had not renewed them. This meant 41% of our
shipments were being booked on spot rates, which are always significantly
higher than contracted rates.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Emergency contract renewal with our top 4 carriers. Rates locked in
          for 12 months. Negotiated hard given our volume.
Step 2 — Brought spot bookings down below 15% of total volume.
Step 3 — Negotiated a fuel surcharge cap clause into all new contracts — capped
          at 10% above the base rate.
Step 4 — Did a lane-by-lane cost analysis. Found 6 lanes where we could
          consolidate shipments and reduce trip frequency.
Step 5 — Introduced LTL consolidation for low-volume lanes.

OUTCOME:
Cost per shipment came down to $1,380 within 8 weeks.
Estimated annual savings of $2.1 million compared to staying on spot rates.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 8 weeks

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- A carrier contract renewal calendar with a 90-day advance reminder.
- If spot bookings in any week exceed 20%, procurement head is automatically
  alerted.
- Monthly freight cost dashboard is now reviewed by CFO and logistics head
  together.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-008
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 20 May 2024
Reported By        : Warehouse Manager, Central DC
KPI Affected       : Inbound Velocity (Dock to Stock Time)
Severity           : HIGH
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
Dock to stock time shot up from 4.2 hours to 11.7 hours over 2 days. At the
peak of the problem, 38 trucks were waiting more than 3 hours just to get to
an unloading dock. Carrier partners started calling to complain.

ROOT CAUSE:
Three receiving dock doors were offline for scheduled maintenance. The
maintenance was originally supposed to take 2 days but got extended to 7 days
because replacement parts were delayed. At the same time, one of our large
suppliers, SUP-204, sent 3 unplanned truckloads without prior appointment —
double their normal weekly volume.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Redirected inbound trucks to use the outbound dock during the morning
          window (6AM to 10AM) when outbound activity is low.
Step 2 — Extended receiving shift to 24 hours for 1 week and brought in
          additional staff.
Step 3 — Called SUP-204 and asked them to hold 2 of their 3 unplanned trucks
          for the following week.
Step 4 — Sourced the maintenance replacement parts from a local vendor to
          speed up completion.

OUTCOME:
Dock to stock time came back to 4.8 hours within 6 days.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 6 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- All dock maintenance must be scheduled during mid-month, which is our
  lowest-volume window.
- Supplier appointment scheduling is now mandatory. Unannounced deliveries
  will be asked to wait up to 24 hours.
- Maximum 2 dock doors allowed to be offline at the same time.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-009
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 8 August 2024
Reported By        : Quality Control Team
KPI Affected       : Receiving Accuracy
Severity           : CRITICAL
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
Receiving accuracy crashed to 71.3% — against our target of 95%. During cycle
counts, we were finding inventory in completely wrong locations. Wrong items
were being put away in wrong bins. The inbound zone was essentially becoming
unreliable.

ROOT CAUSE:
We had hired a batch of temporary workers for the peak season. They had not
completed put-away SOP training properly. To work faster, they were skipping
the barcode scan confirmation step. This was causing systematic misplacements
and nobody had caught it for several days.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Immediately stopped all temp worker put-away activity.
Step 2 — Ran an emergency 2-hour training session on the barcode scan
          protocol — every temp worker attended.
Step 3 — Paired one permanent staff member with every 3 temp workers to
          supervise until accuracy recovered.
Step 4 — Did a full location audit of the inbound zone — found and corrected
          214 misplaced items.
Step 5 — Worked with IT to enforce mandatory scan confirmation in the WMS —
          system now cannot complete a put-away transaction without the scan.

OUTCOME:
Receiving accuracy recovered to 93.8% within 1 week and 96.2% the week after.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 10 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- Temp workers must pass a practical receiving accuracy test before working
  independently in the inbound zone.
- Scan confirmation is now permanently system-enforced — cannot be bypassed.
- Random spot-check of 50 locations done daily to catch issues early.

INCIDENT REPORT — REF: OPS-2024-011
--------------------------------------
Date of Incident   : 2 December 2024
Reported By        : Demand Planning Team Lead
KPI Affected       : Stockout Rate, Days of Cover
Severity           : CRITICAL
Status             : RESOLVED

WHAT HAPPENED:
Seven of our high-velocity SKUs went into stockout simultaneously. Days of
cover had hit zero. We had to cancel or back-order customer orders on these
items. Estimated lost revenue was Rs. 2.8 Crore. This was one of the worst
stockout events we have had in 3 years.

ROOT CAUSE:
The demand plan for Q4 was built using last year's Q4 as the baseline. What
nobody had communicated to the planning team was that the sales team had
launched a major promotional campaign for these exact 7 SKUs. The campaign
drove a 60% surge in orders. Sales and planning were not talking to each other.

WHAT WE DID:
Step 1 — Raised emergency purchase orders with suppliers for all 7 SKUs the
          same day. Requested expedited delivery.
Step 2 — Used air freight for 3 SKUs where sea freight lead time was too long.
Step 3 — Communicated back-order timelines to all affected customers within
          48 hours — proactively, before they called us.
Step 4 — Asked the sales team to pause the promotions on these 7 SKUs until
          stock was replenished.
Step 5 — Allocated whatever remaining stock we had to highest-priority
          customers based on order value and relationship.

OUTCOME:
5 of the 7 SKUs were restocked within 10 days.
2 SKUs took 22 days due to supplier lead time constraints.

TIME TO RESOLVE: 22 days

WHAT WE PUT IN PLACE AFTER THIS:
- A weekly S&OP meeting is now mandatory. Sales, planning, and procurement
  must all attend. No exceptions.
- Any marketing campaign expecting a demand impact of more than 15% must be
  submitted to the demand planning team at least 4 weeks in advance.
- Safety stock for all Tier-1 SKUs recalculated using a 95% service level.
